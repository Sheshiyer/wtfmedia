## Findings and recommended strategy

Build one authenticated, D1-backed production record and show it as both a shared calendar and a pipeline board. A calendar mark should open the underlying item, its planned/actual dates, owner, status, and blockers. Preserve the workbook as a complete, access-controlled source snapshot with cell-level provenance; promote only reconciled records into the operational model. Unresolved marks remain visible with an “unverified source” label until the team resolves them, not silently discarded or guessed.

The existing `/ops/production` and `/beta/workspace/production` screen is a browser-only sketch, not a working database calendar. Its month grid and board can be reused, but its pins must not be presented as saved data. The temporary public-calendar D1 table is also not the episode/dubbing source of truth. Plan the team feature in the Beta operator-auth lane and use the existing `episodes` IDs where records can be confidently matched. Do not mock future operational records: empty states should be honest, imported items should carry source references, and the UI must clearly distinguish planned, actual, and unverified dates.

Two assumptions need correction before implementation:

1. The workbook has **six visible sheets but only one formula-driven month calendar**, with a selector for April 2026–March 2027. The saved file caches August 2026; the other eleven month grids need to be reconstructed and verified, not treated as separately stored sheets.
2. Nikhai has now identified five Nikhil Kamath IPs—WTF Is (panel), WTF Finance, WTF Online, WTF People (one-to-one), and Stitched—plus POV, Animated IP, BTS (All Things WTF), and Ways Of The World. Keep `IP`, content format, and brand/parent separate; allow the team to add other IPs later. Social-media and YouTube distribution are context, not additional IPs or required calendar labels. Never infer an IP or status solely from a sticker color.

## Calendar and daily workflow

- **Default month view:** prev/next month and jump-to-month/year; cover every source month and historical dates, not just the current fiscal year. Each day shows compact, separately colored/labeled chips for shoots, planned posts, actual posts and dubbing events. Overflow opens an accessible day list. Source-only/ambiguous marks show a visible review badge.
- **Filters and alternate views:** IP, format, event kind, production/dubbing status, owner, planned versus actual, and open blockers; week view for near-term coordination and list view for dense days, search and export. Remember filters per user but keep shared data canonical.
- **Item details:** clicking a chip shows title, IP/format, guest(s) subject to role, dates, owner, stage, dubbing and permission summary, blockers, next action, source and edit history. Edits update the same record shown in the board; no browser-only pins or fake cards.
- **Editable legend:** admins or designated WTF editors can rename/recolor IPs and event/status markers, with readable labels/icons and a preview. The original workbook’s blue/green/yellow legend is retained as a **source legend**, not imposed as the future IP scheme. Warn when two active labels become visually indistinguishable.
- **Daily replacement for the wall calendar:** an editor records or moves a shoot/post once, confirms whether it is planned or actual, and assigns owner/next action. Stakeholders such as investment/live-stream teams see the current filtered month or list without waiting for Aditi’s email. Exceptions and overdue blockers appear in a concise “needs attention” view. Printable/exportable views can follow user testing, not drive the data model.

## Dubbing pipeline

For each episode/language: identify participating guests → request/record each guest’s dubbing permission (including sample or no-response) → mark eligible or blocked with a reason/owner → track dub work and received file → verify ready → schedule and record trailer/full Hindi publication. Show separate **Pending**, **Blocked**, **Ready**, **Dubbed**, and **Pushed out** views. Every blocked card states whose permission or what other dependency is missing and the next action; an override needs an authorized actor and audit reason. Do not infer guest permission from an onboarding contract, `Posted? = 1`, or a date in `Dub Episode Schedule`.

The call deferred detailed dubbing design while a separate tracker was evolving, but Nikhai has now explicitly said to ignore the ElevenLabs tracker. Build the local dubbing workflow from the provided workbook and confirmed requirements only. Its missing “dubbed” completion values must start as unknown rather than be fabricated; staff can fill them in through the new tracker. No ElevenLabs integration or dependency is planned.

## Lossless migration and acceptance

1. **Freeze and inventory:** obtain an owner-approved copy/version, checksum it, enumerate all six sheets, populated/formula/merged cells, validation lists, fills/conditional formatting, icons and date systems. Retain a private source snapshot outside Git and a restricted source-table viewer in-platform. Do not place guest contacts or raw workbook data in the repo.
2. **Reconstruct months:** evaluate `Calendar!B1` for April 2026–March 2027 from the source date ranges and preserve the source legend, each month/day and all marks. Compare rendered months with the formula behavior and the cached August sample. Reconcile photo stickers manually where legible; retain unmatched photo observations as unverified evidence rather than inventing events.
3. **Stage mappings:** map explicit numeric dates first; classify text dates, 0/1 flags, colors and abbreviations without guessing. Keep the two prospect lists distinct in source storage, then deduplicate operational people/items by reviewed identity. Use section-aware solo/panel grouping; do not fill down episode titles through solo rows. Quarantine shifted `Dubbing Tracker!C30:D30`, status-only podcast rows 17–19, and conflicting podcast/dubbing flags. Preserve every original cell and mapping decision.
4. **Review queue:** Aditi/owners resolve uncertain IP/format, event type, planned-versus-actual dates, missing years, permissions and episode links. One source entry may map to multiple deliverables or remain source-only. Present per-sheet and per-month counts before approval.
5. **Dry run, then controlled import:** reconcile existing D1 episodes and deployed schema, back up the target, run an idempotent import in an authorized staging environment, verify no unexpected overwrites and only then plan a separately approved production promotion. Re-running the same version must not duplicate events. Keep rollback/re-import receipts.
6. **Acceptance:** compare all populated source rows/cells, all 12 reconstructed month views, event counts by IP/month/type, multiple same-day markers, guest/episode joins, permission counts and unresolved-queue totals against the source. Spot-check each month plus every exception class with Aditi; prove every source entry is either linked to a visible record or visible in the restricted source view. Test role access and that edits persist after refresh on real D1, not fixtures.

“All workbook data migrated” means **losslessly preserved and inspectable inside the protected platform**, not that every prospect name, target count or vague potential date becomes a public-facing calendar event. This avoids false schedule commitments and protects contacts while meeting the no-loss requirement.

## Roles, security and scope

Use the existing Clerk identity plus D1 operator roster/capabilities. Proposed roles: **viewer** can read permitted calendar/board summaries; **editor** can create/update operational events, status and blockers; **calendar admin** can manage legend, import review and scoped permissions. Server-side checks and actor audit apply to every read/write, not merely navigation hiding. Guest contact and permission evidence require narrower access than a general calendar card. Agree exactly which WTF staff and cross-team viewers get each role before rollout. Legacy public `/ops/*` must not leak protected guest/permission details; prefer the authenticated Beta route as the canonical team entry point.

MVP is the shared cross-IP calendar, canonical board, source-preserving import, workbook-based dubbing dependencies, editable legend and role-safe editing, all runnable locally against local D1 with real imported records rather than invented fixtures. Animation uses the common item/event workflow until Sai and Amal specify additional stages. Later work: optional notifications, exports and external calendar projection. A personal task calendar, general CRM for the dream guest list, contract/finance workflow, ElevenLabs integration and two-way Google Calendar sync are **not implied by this request**. Existing roadmap Phase 6 is planned/inactive; approval of this document should explicitly authorize a local build of that slice, not remote migration, deployment or GitHub push.
